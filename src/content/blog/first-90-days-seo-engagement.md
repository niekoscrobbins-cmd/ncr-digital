Ninety days is a reasonable first checkpoint for an SEO engagement: long enough for early technical fixes to show measurable movement, short enough to catch a bad fit before too much budget is spent. What happens inside those 90 days says more about a vendor than the pitch does.

## A Realistic Timeline

| Window | What should happen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1-2 | Technical audit, baseline measurement recorded, tracking instrumented | You can't demonstrate improvement against a number nobody wrote down |
| Weeks 2-4 | Highest-priority technical fixes shipped and verified live | Fast to implement, fast to verify, and they cap the ceiling of everything else |
| Weeks 4-8 | Content or authority work begins, informed by the audit's findings | Should follow the technical foundation, not run in parallel blind to it |
| Weeks 8-12 | First real ranking and traffic movement becomes visible | Google needs time to recrawl, reindex, and re-evaluate before movement shows up |
| Week 12+ | 90-day review against the original baseline | The only way to know if the engagement is actually working |

## What Good Looks Like at Each Stage

**The audit comes first, and it's specific to your site.** If a vendor starts publishing content or building links in week one without having audited crawlability, indexation, and Core Web Vitals, they're building on a foundation they haven't checked. See [what a technical audit should actually surface](/insights/what-is-crawl-budget-saas/) before any other work starts.

**A baseline gets recorded, not assumed.** Whatever the success metric is (indexed pages, ranking position, converting traffic), it needs a documented starting number in week one. Without it, a 90-day review has nothing honest to compare against.

**Early technical wins are real, but small in isolation.** Fixing an indexation problem or a Core Web Vitals score is fast to ship and fast to verify, which makes it a reasonable first deliverable. It's not, by itself, evidence the whole engagement is working — it's evidence the vendor can execute.

**Content and authority work follows the audit, not the calendar.** If a vendor's content calendar was built before the technical audit finished, it wasn't built from your site's actual gaps.

## Warning Signs at the 90-Day Mark

No baseline exists to compare against. No technical audit was delivered in the first month. Every update is qualitative ("things are looking good") rather than tied to the metrics agreed at the start. Any of these is a sign the engagement isn't being run against a real measurement discipline.
