There's no universally correct way to staff SEO work. The right model depends on how much senior technical attention the problem needs versus how much simultaneous capacity the business needs, and those two needs pull in different directions.

## The Four Models Compared

| Model | Cost structure | Senior attention per hour | Capacity | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-house hire | Fixed salary + benefits | High, if the hire is senior | Limited to one person's bandwidth | Companies needing daily coordination with product/engineering |
| Large agency | Retainer, often tiered by scope | Lower — junior staff typically execute | High — can run content, links, and technical work in parallel | Companies needing volume across multiple channels at once |
| Boutique firm | Project or retainer, scoped per engagement | High — smaller client rosters mean more senior time per client | Moderate | Companies with a specific technical problem needing deep, hands-on diagnosis |
| Freelancer | Hourly or project-based | Varies widely by individual | Low — single person, no backup | Small sites or narrowly scoped one-time projects |

## What Each Model Is Actually Built For

**In-house** gives you full-time context on your business and immediate availability for coordination with other teams, at the cost of being a single point of failure. If your one SEO hire leaves, institutional knowledge leaves with them.

**Large agencies** are built for volume: managing many clients across content, links, and technical work simultaneously. That scale usually means the work is distributed across junior staff, with a senior strategist overseeing rather than executing.

**Boutique firms** trade capacity for depth. A smaller roster of clients means more senior attention per engagement, which matters most when the problem is genuinely technical rather than a volume-of-content problem. This is the model behind [NCR Digital's SEO work](/seo): a small client roster by design, so the same person who diagnoses the [crawl budget or indexation problem](/insights/what-is-crawl-budget-saas/) is the one doing the fix.

**Freelancers** offer the lowest hourly cost and the least redundancy. For a narrowly scoped project (a one-time technical audit, a migration review) that's often the right tradeoff. For ongoing work where availability matters, the lack of backup capacity becomes a real risk.

## How the Right Choice Changes Over Time

Most companies outgrow their first model within a year to eighteen months, as the underlying question shifts from "do we have any SEO strategy at all" to "can this scale with the business." A freelancer who was right for an early-stage site's first audit may not be the right fit once the site has thousands of pages and a real crawl-budget problem. Revisit the choice when the scope of the problem changes, not on a fixed schedule.
